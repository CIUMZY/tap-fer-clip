## What the unit is

The unit of the retraining analysis is the **training seed**. Every arm is built and trained inside a seeded context, so its initial weights, its batch order and (in the nested condition) its learning rate follow from the seed alone; the arms of one seed share the random stream, and rebuilding an arm from its seed gives a bit-identical checkpoint. Ten seeds (5-14) are used at ViT-B/16 and fifteen (5-19) at ViT-L/14, and every arm is trained at both points of the original learning-rate grid.

## Multiplicity

Family = the six contrasts above. Sign-test p-values: 0.1094, 0.6072, 0.3438, 0.6072, 0.7539, 0.0001.

| correction | adjusted p (same order) | rejections |
|---|---|---|
| Holm-Bonferroni | 0.5469, 1.0000, 1.0000, 1.0000, 1.0000, 0.0004 | 1 |
| Benjamini-Hochberg | 0.3281, 0.7287, 0.6875, 0.7287, 0.7539, 0.0004 | 1 |

One contrast survives both corrections: the ViT-L/14 degradation margin against CLIP-Adapter, 15/15 seeds positive (adjusted p = 0.0004). The correction matters: with five seeds the exact two-sided sign test cannot fall below 0.0625, so a five-seed version of this study could not have separated the survivor from the rest.

## What the intervals do and do not cover

- The t and sign-test intervals cover variation across **training seeds**: they answer what a different run of the same pipeline would have produced.
- The seed-level bootstrap resamples the 10 or 15 seeds; the unit-level bootstrap resamples evaluation units inside a fixed seed. The two answer different questions and both are reported.
- Neither covers a different corpus, a different encoder checkpoint, a different adaptation architecture, or a change in the preprocessing. The scope of this study is facial expression recognition with two CLIP encoders and three adaptation arms.
- The Set B contrast is computed from three targets, so its per-seed statistic rests on three scoring units; that axis cannot support a cell-level interval and we do not claim one.
